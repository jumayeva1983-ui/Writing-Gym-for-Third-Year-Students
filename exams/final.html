Task 1: real-life writing

30 min · 20 points

Choose one variant. Write 150–200 words. You are marked on how well you answer the task, a register that suits the reader, the word count, and grammatical accuracy.

Variant A · formal email

You bought a three-month membership at Chorsu Sports Centre (fictional). The swimming pool, the main reason you joined, has been closed for three weeks, and nobody told the members. Write to the manager, Mr Bobur Saidov. Explain the problem, say how it affects you, and ask for a solution by a clear deadline.

Variant B · review for a student website

A new café has opened near your faculty and advertises itself as “the perfect place to study”. Write a review for the student union website. Describe the place, judge whether the claim is true, and give a clear recommendation to other students.

Feedback: what a strong answer does

Variant A

Subject line → purpose in the first sentence → facts (membership dates, three weeks, no notice) → effect on you → one clear request (an extension or a partial refund) with a date → polite, firm closing. Dear Mr Saidov … Yours sincerely. No anger, no capitals, no !!!

Variant B

Title → what, where, and for whom → specific evidence (noise, Wi-Fi, sockets, prices, opening hours) → a fair verdict on the “perfect place to study” claim → a recommendation for a particular kind of student.

Task 2: academic essay

45 min · 30 points

Choose one variant and write an academic essay of 250–300 words: an introduction with a thesis, body paragraphs with relevant examples, and a conclusion. Plan first: the plan is not counted.

Variant A

Some people argue that university students should be assessed only through coursework, not through examinations. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Variant B

Machine translation is now so good that students of foreign languages no longer need to learn to write in them. To what extent do you agree?

Self-check
Support with relevant examples10 pts

Each point is backed by a specific, relevant example or piece of evidence.

Grammar and vocabulary10 pts

Accurate grammar; precise academic vocabulary; formal register.

Logic and coherence10 pts

A clear thesis; one idea per paragraph; signposts that show how the ideas connect; a conclusion that follows from the body.

Feedback: what examiners look for

Variant A, a possible plan

Thesis: a mix of coursework and examinations is fairer than either alone. Point 1: coursework rewards steady work and research skills (example: a term project). Point 2: examinations show what a student can do alone and under time limits, which coursework cannot prove in the age of AI tools. Conclusion: keep both, with clear weights.

Variant B, a possible plan

Thesis: I largely disagree. Point 1: a translation tool cannot choose the right register for a reader the student has not analysed. Point 2: checking a machine translation needs the very writing skills it is said to replace. Concession: tools help with drafts and vocabulary. Conclusion: learn to write, then use the tools wisely.

Common slips

No thesis in the introduction, examples that are general (“many people think”), a conclusion that brings in a new idea, and an informal tone (you, contractions, questions to the reader).
