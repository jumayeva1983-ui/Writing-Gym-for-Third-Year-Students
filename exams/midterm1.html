Paragraph writing

20 min · 25 points

Write one well-organised paragraph on one of the topics. It needs a clear topic sentence, details that support it, and a sentence that concludes it.

  1. The benefits of studying in a group rather than alone.
  2. Online news has replaced printed newspapers for young people. Do you agree?
  3. Describe a skill you learned outside the classroom and how it has helped you.
My topic:
Word count:0
Self-check
Topic sentence5 pts

States one clear main idea that answers the topic.

Supporting details8 pts

Two or three points, each explained or illustrated with a specific example.

Concluding sentence4 pts

Closes the paragraph without simply repeating the first sentence.

Length3 pts

150–180 words.

Accuracy and register5 pts

Neutral or formal throughout; few errors, none that block meaning.

Feedback: what a strong paragraph does

Shape

Topic sentence → point 1 + example → point 2 + example → (point 3) → concluding sentence that adds a final thought.

Example topic sentence (a)

Studying in a group helps many students learn more, because they explain ideas to each other, share the work, and keep one another motivated.

Common slips

A topic sentence that only names the topic (“This paragraph is about groups”), points without examples, and a final sentence that copies the first.

Error correction

10 min · 15 points

The paragraph contains five grammatical or stylistic errors. Two sentences are correct. Find the five sentences with errors and write each one out correctly. Each correct sentence = 3 points.

Corrected sentences15 pts

Count a sentence only if the error is fixed and no new error appears.

Answers

The five errors

  1. organise → organised (past tense: last spring)
  2. forty volunteer → forty volunteers (plural after a number)
  3. were → was (the subject is every visitor, singular)
  4. a real success, the money → a real success, and the money / success; the money (comma splice)
  5. encourage → encouraging (three adjectives in a list)

Correct as written

Sentences 3 and 7.

Summarising a text

15 min · 20 points

Read the text (114 words). Summarise it in your own words in 60–80 words; avoid lifting whole sentences.

Learning with podcasts

Podcasts have become a popular way for university students to study a foreign language outside class. Unlike textbooks, they let learners hear real speakers with different accents and speeds, and they can be played while walking or travelling. However, listening alone does not guarantee progress. Teachers point out that many learners play episodes in the background and remember very little afterwards. The most effective listeners treat a podcast as a lesson: they choose episodes slightly above their level, listen more than once, note down useful phrases, and later try to use these phrases when they speak or write. Used in this active way, a short daily episode can build both vocabulary and listening confidence.

Word count:0

Self-check
Main idea6 pts

Podcasts help only when they are used actively.

Key supporting points6 pts

Why podcasts are useful, the problem of passive listening, and what active listeners do.

Own words5 pts

No copied strings; the copy check above helps.

Length and accuracy3 pts

60–80 words, with few errors.

Feedback: a model summary

Model (67 words)

According to the text, podcasts are a convenient tool for language students because they offer authentic voices and accents and can be used on the move. Their value, however, depends on how they are used. Learners who leave episodes running in the background gain little. Those who pick slightly difficult material, replay it, record new expressions, and then practise them improve their vocabulary and their listening confidence.

Check

Did you keep the contrast (however) between passive and active listening? It is the heart of the text.

Sentence transformation

15 min · 20 points

Change each sentence into the form shown in brackets. 4 points per item.

1. (Active → passive) The committee approved the new timetable on Monday.

→

2. (Simple → complex) The library was closed. We studied in the café.

→

3. (Direct → indirect speech) She said, “I am preparing my presentation now.”

→

4. (Add a hedging expression) Online exams are less reliable than paper exams.

→

5. (Add a topic sentence) …………… First, a part-time job teaches students to manage their time. Second, it gives them experience of dealing with real customers. Finally, the money they earn can reduce the pressure on their families.

→
Self-check
Correct transformations20 pts

The structure must change as asked and the meaning must stay the same.

Answers
  1. The new timetable was approved (by the committee) on Monday.
  2. Because / As / Since the library was closed, we studied in the café.
  3. She said (that) she was preparing her presentation then / at that moment.
  4. Online exams may be / appear to be / are likely to be less reliable than paper exams.
  5. A part-time job can benefit university students in several ways.

Watch out

In 3, the tense moves back and now changes. In 4, one hedge is enough: “may possibly perhaps” is over-hedging. In 5, the topic sentence must cover all three points, not only the first.

Self-check
Correct answers (label and feature)20 pts

Count an answer only if the label is right and the feature really shows it.

Feedback: features
  1. Informal: Hey!, contractions (Can’t), the subject left out, a question to a friend.
  2. Formal: passive voice (are requested, will not be considered), formal phrases such as no later than.
  3. Formal: academic vocabulary (findings), hedging (suggest, may), no personal pronouns.
  4. Informal: guess what?, an idiom (over the moon), a contraction, an exclamation mark.
