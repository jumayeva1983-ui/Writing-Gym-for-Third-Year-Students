Today's one job: put the purpose in the subject line and the first two lines, so a busy reader sees it in ten seconds.

Look!

~3 min

Two emails from Malika to the same lecturer. This is all the reader sees in the inbox. Which one will be opened first?

Before: the reader guesses

Subject: Hi
Hello! Hope you are well and everything is fine. My name is Malika and I am from…

After: the reader knows

Subject: Request to submit my essay on 12 May
Dear Dr Rahimova, I am writing to ask whether I could submit my essay on 12 May instead of 9 May.

Spot it: the subject line names the topic and the date; the first sentence states the request. The details can come later.

!

The rule: bottom line up front

SEVERAL QUESTIONS?→number them: 1, 2, 3

Order it!

~4 min

Aziz's email to the facilities office is mixed up. Put the parts in order: 1 to 7.

Could you please confirm by Friday, 14 May, whether the hall is available?
Subject: Booking request: Main Hall, 21 May, 14:00–17:00
Kind regards, Aziz Karimov, Chair, English Debate Club
I am writing to request the Main Hall for the final of our English debate competition on 21 May, from 14:00 to 17:00.

Email corner

~25 min

THE SITUATION

English Club · Poetry evening

  • The club wants to hold a poetry evening in English in the faculty library
  • Possible dates: 26 or 28 May, 16:00–18:00; about 40 guests
  • You need: permission, chairs, a microphone, and to know if tea is allowed

Write to the head librarian, Ms Feruza Nazarova. You have never written to her before.

Your turn. Write two emails.
Email A: your request to Ms Nazarova. Use numbered questions. Target: 100–130 words.
Email B: a polite follow-up, five working days later, because she has not replied. Target: 50–70 words.
