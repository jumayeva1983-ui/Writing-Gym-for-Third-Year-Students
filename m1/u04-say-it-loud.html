Say It Loud

writing opinion articles

Unit 4

Today's one job: say what you think in one clear sentence, give reasons, and answer the other side.

0

Look!

~3 min

The city council is asking: should the old town centre be closed to cars on Sundays? Three people answer.

A feeling

Cars in the centre are SO annoying!!!

An opinion

The old centre should be closed to cars on Sundays.

An argument

The old centre should be closed to cars on Sundays, because families could walk there safely and small shops would gain customers.

Spot it: an argument = claim + because + reason. Feelings shout; arguments explain.

!

The rule: the op-ed train

HOOK→a scene, a question, a number, a bold claim: make them read on
THESIS→your position in one sentence, in paragraph 1 or 2: arguable, specific, focused
REASONS→2–3 paragraphs: point sentence + reason + example or evidence
THEY SAY / I SAY→the strongest objection → admit what is fair → answer it
ACTION→what should happen next? End with energy; echo the hook

Your voice: not too soft, not too loud

Hedges soften

may, might, perhaps, it seems, in many cases. Use them for what you cannot prove.

A car-free day may reduce noise.

Boosters strengthen

clearly, certainly, in fact, there is no doubt that. Use them only for what you can prove.

This clearly saves money.

Circle fact, opinion (no reason) or argument (claim + reason). Then press the red pen.

2

Hook hunter

~5 min

Six hooks for an op-ed on car-free Sundays. Choose the type of each hook: scene, question, bold claim, imagine, contrast, number.

Last Sunday, I waited four minutes to cross the main square with my grandmother.
When did you last hear birds in the city centre?
Cars have no place in a city's oldest streets.
Picture the main square on a Sunday morning: children on bicycles, tables outside the cafés, no horns.
We built the old centre for people; now we only drive through it.
In one hour last Sunday, our class counted 214 cars crossing the old square.

Which hook would make you read on? Write one more hook of your own on the same topic.

Thesis doctor

~4 min

Diagnose each thesis. Only two are healthy.

1. In this article, I will discuss car-free Sundays.////
2. Pollution is bad for health.////
3. The old town centre should be closed to cars every Sunday from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., starting this summer.////
4. Cities have many problems with transport.////
5. Our university should give every first-year student a free bus pass for the first semester.////
6. Some people like cars, and some people do not.////
4

They say, I say

~6 min

Put the four sentences of this counterargument paragraph in order (1–4): they say → admit → answer → I say.

However, the shops in the centre depend on people who walk past, not on people who drive past.
Some shop owners fear that a car-free day will keep their customers away.
For this reason, a car-free Sunday would help local businesses rather than harm them.
It is true that some customers come by car to carry heavy shopping.

Your turn. Objection: “Car-free Sundays will simply create traffic jams in the streets around the centre.” Write a counterargument paragraph (3–4 sentences): they say → admit → answer → I say.

5

Voice control

~6 min

Each sentence is too loud, too soft or unfair. Rewrite it so that it sounds confident and fair.

Three tricks:
1. Boost only what you can prove.
2. Hedge what you cannot prove, but do not hedge twice.
3. Criticise ideas, never people.

1. Everybody knows that cars are destroying our city.

→Traffic is damaging the oldest streets of our city.

(example)

2. It might maybe be possible that a car-free day could perhaps help a little.

→

3. Without any doubt, all drivers will love this idea.

→

4. I am not sure, but maybe students should possibly get cheaper bus tickets.

→

5. Only selfish people drive in the old town.

→

6. This is absolutely the best idea in the history of our city.
