Fix It, Fund It!

proposals for community or work

Unit 6

Today's one job: turn a problem into a plan that a busy decision-maker can say yes to.

0

Look!

~3 min

Students want bicycle racks outside the faculty. Three ways to ask the faculty administration. Which one gets a yes?

A wish

We really need bike racks. It is a very actual problem. Please help us!!

A problem with evidence

Since September, about 40 students have cycled to the faculty every day. There is nowhere to lock a bike, and six bikes have been stolen this semester.

A plan

We propose four racks for 20 bicycles beside the main entrance, installed by the maintenance team by 1 April. The total cost is 3.2 million soums.

Spot it: a wish has no evidence, no plan, no cost. A proposal needs all three.

!

The rule: four stops to a yes

SITUATION
background→What is the context? Who, where, what has changed?
PROBLEM
with evidence→What is wrong? Show it: a source, numbers, examples.
SOLUTION
objectives + plan→What exactly do you propose? Who does what, when?
EVALUATION
budget + benefits→What will it cost? Why will it work? How will you measure it?

Objectives: make them SMART

S→specific: what exactly, for whom?
M→measurable: a number you can count
A→assignable / achievable: who is responsible? can it be done?
R→realistic / relevant: possible with these resources?
T→time-related: by when?

Think in Uzbek

dolzarb masala → an urgent / pressing issue
(“actual problem” emas: actual = haqiqiy)

loyihani amalga oshirish → carry out / implement a project
(“realise a project” emas)

Writing corner

~25 min

WATER FOR EVERYONE?

Your notes after a week of looking around the faculty

  • One drinking-water point for about 900 students and staff
  • Students buy plastic bottles every day; the bins are full by noon
  • Idea: three refill stations (ground, 2nd and 4th floors)
  • A local supplier's estimate: 4.5 million soums in total

Reader: the faculty's deputy dean for administration.

Your turn. Write two parts of a proposal.
Part A: the summary: what you propose, where, when and at what cost (40–60 words).
Part B: the problem statement and two SMART objectives (100–140 words).
You may invent plausible details, such as survey figures, but keep them realistic.
