Cut the Fluff

editing for precision

Unit 15

Today's one job: say it once, say it exactly, and make every word point to one thing.

0

Look!

~3 min

Three volunteers wrote the first line of a report on the Language Café, a weekly English conversation club. Only one line tells the reader exactly what happened.

Fluff

“The Language Café, which was a new innovation, first began its weekly meetings every Wednesday.”

Fog

“A lot of people came and it was really quite good, so this shows we should do more.”

Precise

“From 4 February to 25 March, the Language Café met eight times, with an average of 23 students per session.”

Spot it: the precise line says each thing once, gives dates and numbers, and has no word that could point to two things.

!

The rule: four checks and a final one

SAY IT ONCE→cut what the reader can inferfree gift → giftround in shape → roundfirst began → began
SAY IT EXACTLY→a lot of → 31 of 40soon → by 10 Mayan issue → name it
POINT CLEARLY→one it / they = one nounThis → This decision, This increase
PUT IT IN PLACE→only, almost: right before their wordan -ing opening = the subject who does it
LIGHTEN→unpack noun stackscut of-chainsnot un… → say it positively

Think in Uzbek

Maʼlumotlarni yigʻib, jadval tuzildi.
Having collected the data, a table was made.
→ Having collected the data, we made a table.

Bu esa … → This … → This change …

koʻp talabalar → 31 of 40 students

Danger zone

a lot of, very, thing, issue ✗
This shows … with no noun ✗
Having finished, the room was cleaned. ✗
The reason why … is because … ✗
café / club / meeting / event for the same thing ✗

Which is more precise?

~3 min

Which sentence is more precise: A or B?

1. A The café was very popular.
B Attendance averaged 23 students per session./
2. A Most of the 40 members came at least five times.
B Lots of members came quite often./
3. A We need more chairs soon.
B We need ten more chairs by 6 May./
4. A Two issues came up.
B Two problems came up: the hall was noisy, and the first session started late./
5. A The feedback was really good.
B Thirty-four of the 38 guests rated the sessions 4 or 5 out of 5./
6. A The speaking tasks were shortened in week 3.

Writing corner

~20 min

Your turn. The paragraph below comes from the Language Café report for the dean's office, which will decide whether to fund a second weekly session. Use the fact card to make it precise. Target: 80–120 words.

FACT CARD

Language Café · spring term (fictional data for practice)

  • 8 sessions, Wednesdays 16:00–17:00, 4 February – 25 March
  • 184 visits in total (average 23 per session); 15 students on a waiting list
  • Weeks 1–2 in the main hall: too noisy → moved to Room 214 in week 3
  • Room 214 has 14 chairs; the library lent 10 more
  • Feedback: 34 of 38 guests rated the sessions 4 or 5 out of 5
  • Request: a second session on Fridays 15:00–16:00 from 17 April; needs Room 214 and two volunteers

“The Language Café was a very big success, and lots of students came during the period of the spring term. At the start there were some issues with the hall, which was a problem, so we moved. Having moved to Room 214, there were not enough chairs, but they lent us some. This shows that more sessions are needed in the future, and we plan to ask the dean's office about it soon, as many students are waiting.”
