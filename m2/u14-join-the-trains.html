Writing Gym · Module 2 · Unit 14

Join the Trains

writing complex sentences

Unit 14

Today's one job: couple your ideas with the right link, so the reader sees how they fit together.

0

Look!

~3 min

A student wrote the same three ideas about her morning commute in three ways. Which version would you like to read in an essay?

Choppy

The 8:10 train was cancelled. I reached campus at ten. I missed the statistics quiz.

Overloaded

The 8:10 train was cancelled and so I reached campus at ten and I missed the statistics quiz and so I had to email the tutor because the quiz counts and …

Joined

Because the 8:10 train was cancelled, I reached campus at ten; as a result, I missed the statistics quiz.

Spot it: the joined version keeps the ideas short enough to follow, and each link (because, as a result) tells the reader the relation: cause, then result.

!

The rule: four ways to couple two ideas

AND · BUT · SO · YET→joins two equal clausesclause, but clauseit cannot move
ALTHOUGH · BECAUSE · IF · UNLESS→starts a dependent clauseAlthough clause, clauseclause although clause
HOWEVER · THEREFORE→links meaning, not grammarclause. However, clauseclause; however, clause
DESPITE · BECAUSE OF→+ noun phrase or -ingdespite the cancellationbecause of missing the train

Relative clauses: who, which, that

WHICH ONE?→defining: no commasPassengers who book early pay less.
EXTRA INFO→non-defining: commas, never thatThe station, which opened in 1899, is a museum now.

Which train?

~4 min

Which train is it? S = simple, Cd = compound, Cx = complex, CC = compound-complex. Count the clauses, not the words.

1. Night trains between the two cities run three times a week in summer.///
2. Tickets sell out quickly, so most passengers book a month ahead.///
3. Passengers who travel overnight save the cost of a hotel.///
4. Although the journey takes eleven hours, many travellers prefer it to flying, and the route is growing.///
5. If the new line opens on time, the trip will become four hours shorter.///
6. The old station was repaired over the spring and reopened in June.///
7. The carriages are old; nevertheless, they are clean and comfortable.///
8. When the heating failed, the conductor apologised, and the company refunded part of the fare.///

Commas or no commas?

~3 min

Does the relative clause need commas? Ask: does it tell me which one (no commas), or does it add extra information (commas)?

1. Samarkand which has a new high-speed rail link attracts many tourists./
2. Students who sit at the back often cannot see the slides./
3. The survey that the dean ordered will close on Friday./
4. Dr Rahimova who supervised my project has moved to another university./
5. The bus which leaves at 7:15 is usually half empty./
6. The new reading room which opened in May has 80 seats./

Same meaning, new link. Change the grammar after the link if you need to.

→Despite the rain, the festival went ahead.

Couple the paragraph

~4 min

Put the sentences in order to build one paragraph. The links are your clues: what must come before However, As a result or this fear?

As a result, they keep driving, even for very short trips.
Many cities want more people to cycle to work.
Separate bike lanes, which keep cyclists away from cars, could remove this fear.
However, many commuters feel unsafe on busy roads.
Although such lanes are expensive, they may change travel habits more than advertising does.
Cycling is cheap, and it does not pollute the air.

Writing corner

~20 min

Your turn. This paragraph from a study-skills leaflet is choppy. Join the ideas into a smooth paragraph of 80–140 words. Do not just add and: choose links that show the relations.

Podcasts are popular with language learners. They are free. Learners can listen anywhere. They hear natural speech. Podcasts have limits. Learners cannot ask questions. Some podcasts are too fast for beginners. Transcripts can help. Learners can read and listen at the same time. Teachers can recommend podcasts. The podcasts should match the learners' level.

7. Writing corner

Model (99 words): Podcasts are popular with language learners because they are free and can be played anywhere. They also let learners hear natural speech. However, podcasts have limits. Listeners cannot ask questions, and some programmes are too fast for beginners. Transcripts can help with this problem, since learners can read and listen at the same time. Teachers, who know their students' needs, can also recommend suitable podcasts. Although many good programmes exist, the best choice is one that matches the learner's level, so a short recommendation from a teacher may save students a great deal of time.
