Who Did It?

active, passive and tense

Unit 12

Today's one job: show the reader who when it matters, and give every verb the tense that does its job.

0

Look!

~3 min

Three students described the same step of a small study of traffic noise near the campus (fictional). Which version belongs in a Methods section?

Chatty

“So we got a noise app, went out three times a day and measured stuff.”

Hiding

“Some readings were deleted, and it was decided not to mention this.” Who? Why?

Methods

“Noise was measured with a phone app at three points near the campus at 8:00, 13:00 and 18:00. Two readings were excluded because a lorry had stopped beside the phone with its engine running.”

Spot it: the Methods version uses the passive because the process matters and the doer is obvious, but it still tells the reader why data were removed. No got, no stuff.

!

The rule: who matters, and which job?

PASSIVE→be + past participleThe noise was measured (by …)doer unknown, obvious or unimportant
ACTIVE→the doer is the subjectOur group leader deleted two readings.the reader needs to know who

Three tenses, three jobs

PRESENT→what is knownwhat a source arguesFigure 1 shows …These results suggest …
PAST→one earlier studyyour methodyour results
PRESENT PERFECT→research up to nowthe gap: little research has looked at …

Active or passive?

Read the context in italics. Which continuation, A or B, suits it better? Circle.

1. Methods:
A A volunteer counted the cars, and a classmate wrote the numbers down.
B The cars were counted every five minutes./

A A bicycle was stolen from the faculty car park last night.

3. An email to a customer who is waiting for an order:
A The order was sent to the wrong address.
B I sent your order to the wrong address, and I apologise./
4. The new timetable was introduced in March.
A The dean's office designed it after a staff survey.

5. A museum report:
A The paintings got cleaned before the exhibition.

A Each interview was recorded with the participant's permission.

7. Writing corner

Methods, for example (93 words): Noise levels were measured at three points near the campus: the main gate, the bus stop and the library garden. Readings were taken with a free phone app, using the same phone each time, at 8:00, 13:00 and 18:00 on five weekdays in April. Each reading lasted 10 minutes. All readings were taken by two members of the group, Laylo and Timur, to keep the procedure consistent. Two readings were excluded because a lorry stopped beside the phone with its engine running. The average level was then calculated for each point and time.

Results, for example (26 words): The highest average level, 74 dB, was recorded at the bus stop at 18:00. The lowest, 52 dB, was recorded in the library garden at 13:00.
