Writing Gym · Module 2 · Unit 13

Maybe, Probably, Certainly

balancing hedging and confidence

Unit 13

Today's one job: make every claim exactly as strong as your evidence: not louder, not quieter.

0

Look!

~3 min

A faculty counted how many students came to its lectures. Read the facts, then three ways of discussing them.

Early lectures (fictional data for practice)

In one faculty, 12 lecture courses were observed for a semester. Average attendance was 71 % at the six courses that started at 8:00 and 86 % at the six that started at 10:00.

Too loud

This proves that students are lazy in the morning. Early lectures must be cancelled everywhere.

Too quiet

Perhaps the start time might possibly have had some sort of effect, maybe.

Just right

In this faculty, attendance was clearly lower at 8:00 than at 10:00. This suggests that start time may affect attendance, although the courses also differed in other ways.

Spot it: the good version states the fact firmly, hedges the explanation and limits the claim to this faculty.

!

The rule: observe, interpret, generalise

OBSERVE→what your data showstate it firmlyattendance was lower at 8:00
INTERPRET→what it might meanhedge itsuggests · may · appears to
GENERALISE→beyond your datalimit itin this faculty · tend to · some

The certainty dial

POSSIBLE→might · couldpossiblyseem
PROBABLE→may · probablylikelysuggest · indicate
CERTAIN→clearly · certainlyshow · demonstrateonly when the data really show it

Danger zone

This proves … after one small study ✗
all / always / every without data ✗
might perhaps possibly ✗
approximately 12 courses when you counted them ✗
We can say that … ✗

From maybe to certainly

~3 min

Put the five claims in order starting with the weakest (1) and ending with the strongest (5).

The new bus route probably reduced late arrivals.
There is a slight possibility that the new bus route reduced some late arrivals.
Without any doubt, the new bus route ended all late arrivals.
The new bus route may have reduced late arrivals.
The data clearly show that the new bus route reduced late arrivals.

Match the claim to the evidence

~5 min

Read the evidence. Which claim is exactly as strong as the evidence? Circle a, b or c.

1. Evidence: six of the eight people in a focus group liked the new canteen menu.
a) Everyone loves the new menu.
b) Most members of the focus group liked the new menu.
c) It is perhaps possible that some people might maybe like it.//
2. Evidence: temperature records for 50 years show summers in a (fictional) town getting steadily hotter.
a) The records show a clear rise in summer temperatures in the town.
b) Summers could perhaps possibly be a little warmer, maybe.
c) The records prove that every future summer will be hotter.//
3. Evidence: in one café, tea sales rose in the week when the price fell.
a) Lower prices always increase sales.
b) The price cut caused the rise.
c) The rise may be linked to the lower price, although the weather or other factors could also explain it.//

a) Cyclists may possibly feel slightly safer, perhaps.

7. Writing corner

Model (119 words): In this faculty, attendance was clearly lower at 8:00 lectures than at 10:00 lectures (71 % against 86 %). This suggests that an early start may discourage some students from attending. Travel may be part of the explanation, since more than half of the students surveyed said that a long journey made early lectures difficult. These figures need careful reading, however. The early and later courses differed in year of study and in lecturer, so the start time cannot be separated from these factors, and the data cover only one semester. The faculty could test the effect more directly by moving one or two courses to a later time next semester and comparing attendance before and after the change.
