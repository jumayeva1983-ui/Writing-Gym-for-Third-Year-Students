Look!

~3 min

A (fictional) town voted on a name for its new park: Oak Park 412 votes, River Park 398, Sun Park 120. Two posters showed the result.

Poster 1

“Oak Park wins by a mile!” Only two names are shown, and the axis starts at 390.

Poster 2

Figure 1 shows the votes for the three names. Oak Park and River Park received almost the same number of votes, while Sun Park received far fewer.

Spot it: Poster 1 hides a name and cuts the axis, so a difference of 14 votes looks huge. Poster 2 starts at zero, shows all the data, and its sentence says what to notice.

!

The rule: pick the display from the message

SENTENCE→one to three numbers“18 of 20 finished”
TABLE→exact values to look upmany values, different units
FIGURE→a pattern at a glancebar · line · scatter

Which chart?

BAR→compare categoriesstart at 0, sort by size
LINE→change over time
SCATTER→two measures togetherone dot = one person
PIE→parts of one wholefew parts, total 100 %

The APA label

ABOVE→Table 2 (bold)Title in Italic Title Case
BELOW→Note. N = 50. …

Think in Uzbek

3-jadval → Table 3 (“3-table” emas)

4-rasm → Figure 4 (“Picture 4” emas)

Masofa, km → Distance (km)

talabalar soni → Number of Students

Danger zone

Table №2 ✗
bar chart starting at 390 ✗
3D pie ✗
same numbers in a table AND a chart ✗
“Everyone LOVES Oak!” as a title ✗

8. Writing corner

Figure 1 / Classes Attended Most Often by Members of the Sports Centre / Note. N = 50. Each member chose one class. Fictional data for practice.

Model (86 words): The questionnaire was answered by all 50 members of the sports centre. Figure 1 shows the class that each member attended most often. Swimming was clearly the most popular class, named by 21 members (42%), almost twice as many as yoga, the second most popular class (12 members). Together, these two classes accounted for two thirds of the answers. Football was named by 10 members and chess by only seven. In addition, 38 of the 50 members (76%) said that they would like more evening classes.

Alt text: Horizontal bar chart of the classes that 50 sports centre members attended most often. Swimming was the most popular (21 members), followed by yoga (12), football (10), and chess (7).
