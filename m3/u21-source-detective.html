Look!

~3 min

A student is reviewing research on podcasts for listening practice (fictional sources for practice). Compare three ways of writing about the same three studies.

The shopping list

Halilova (2021) studied podcasts. She found good results. Turgunov (2022) also studied podcasts. Bekova (2023) studied podcasts too.

The fan letter

This article is very interesting and useful. The author is a famous professor, so everything in it is true!

The detective's report

Two classroom studies found that weekly podcast listening improved students' listening scores (Halilova, 2021; Turgunov, 2022). In contrast, Bekova (2023) found no gain when students listened without tasks. No study has yet examined third-year students.

Spot it: the report is built around an idea, not a list of authors. It shows agreement, contrast and what is still unknown, and it puts most names in brackets.

Order the review

~4 min

Put the review paragraph in order (1–5): what is known → agreement → contrast → gap → this study. (fictional sources)

In contrast, Bekova (2023) found no gain when students listened without follow-up tasks.
Podcasts are now a common resource for listening practice outside class.
This study therefore asks how third-year students use podcasts on their own and whether they combine them with tasks.
Two classroom studies reported higher listening scores after weekly podcast lessons (Halilova, 2021; Turgunov, 2022).
However, both studies took place in class, and little is known about how senior students use podcasts on their own.

Teacher's corner: answers

1. Search strings

  1. (podcasts OR "audio files") AND listening
  2. "listening comprehension"
  3. podcasts AND listening NOT children
  4. add another concept with AND
  5. add synonyms with OR

  2. drop (advertisement, no method)
  3. with care (old, no data: background only)

  5. drop (no evidence)
  6. drop (reliable but irrelevant)

Known (Podcasts are now …) → agreement (Two classroom studies …) → contrast (In contrast, …) → gap (However, … little is known …) → this study (This study therefore …).

8. Writing corner

Model (126 words): Halilova (2021) compared two groups of first-year university students over ten weeks: one group listened to a weekly podcast and completed comprehension tasks, while the other followed the usual course. On the same listening test, the podcast group improved more than the comparison group. The study appeared in a peer-reviewed journal and used a pre-test and a post-test, which makes its findings fairly reliable; however, the groups were not randomly assigned, and the author taught both groups. It is only partly relevant to my question, because it examines first-year students in class rather than third-year students on their own. I will use it in my introduction to show that podcasts with tasks can support listening, and in my discussion to compare classroom use with independent use.
