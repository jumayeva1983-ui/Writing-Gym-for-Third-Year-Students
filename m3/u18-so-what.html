Writing Gym · Module 3 · Unit 18

So What?

writing strong thesis statements

Unit 18

Today's one job: turn a topic into a claim worth reading: arguable, specific, and significant.

0

Look!

~3 min

The city council wants to know whether its libraries should stay open later. Three students wrote the first sentence of a report. Compare them.

Just a topic

Evening opening hours of city libraries.

Just a fact

Most libraries in our city close at 6 p.m.

A thesis

Keeping one branch library open until 10 p.m. in examination weeks would give students from crowded homes a quiet place to study, at a far lower cost than building new study halls.

Spot it: the thesis makes a claim someone could question, says who, where, and when, and shows why it matters.

!

The rule: from topic to thesis

TOPIC→a noun phraseevening opening hours
FOCUS→Who? Where?When? Which aspect?
QUESTION→what will I find out?How …? Why …? What …?
THESIS→my answer + why it matters

Three tests for every thesis

ARGUABLE?→could a reasonable reader disagree?
SPECIFIC?→who, what, which aspect?
SO WHAT?→who cares, and why?not too big, not too small

Think in Uzbek

…ga bagʻishlangan → is devoted to
mavzuni emas, daʼvoni yozing: This paper argues that …

…ni oʻrganish → to study
aniq feʼl: to compare, to measure

vazifalar roʻyxati ≠ tezis
bu ish rejasi, xulosa emas

The so-what test

~4 min

Ask So what? Does the thesis matter, is it a so-what shrug, or does it promise too much?

1. Students in our dormitory drink more tea than coffee.//
2. Students who sit in the first three rows ask more questions in seminars, which suggests that seating plans could be used to encourage participation.//
3. My survey of 20 classmates will solve the problem of student motivation in every country.//
4. Most third-year students cannot name their university's rules on plagiarism, so a short session on these rules may be needed before the research module.//
5. The chairs in the reading room are made of wood.//
6. Bilingual menus in campus cafés often contain English spelling errors, which may confuse visitors and could be fixed by a checking service run by language students.//
7. This study of one café will change the way the whole country eats.//

Open or closed?

~3 min

Is the thesis open (the claim only) or closed (the claim + the main points, like a map)?

1. Seminar groups should be limited to 15 students./
2. Seminar groups should be limited to 15 students, because smaller groups let everyone speak, allow individual feedback, and make discussion less stressful./
3. Short daily vocabulary quizzes help students more than one long weekly test./
4. The canteen should offer a cheaper set lunch, because many students skip lunch to save money, hungry students concentrate less, and the canteen would sell more meals./
5. Free evening language clubs improve students' confidence in speaking./
6. Online office hours suit commuting students for two reasons: they save travel time, and they can be booked at short notice./

Down the funnel

~3 min

A student is planning a short study on cycling to campus. Put her notes in order from the widest (1) to the last step (5).

Research question: What reasons do students who live 2–5 km from campus give for not cycling to classes?
Topic: cycling to campus
So what? If so, secure bicycle parking may be a cheaper way to increase cycling than a new cycle lane.
Focus: students of one university who live 2–5 km from campus; this autumn; their reasons for not cycling
Working thesis: Most of these students do not cycle because there is nowhere safe to leave a bicycle, not because of the distance.

7. Writing corner

Model (83 words): Many students buy new textbooks every semester, although senior students often keep copies they no longer need. This study asks how many second-year students in one faculty would use a free textbook exchange run by the student council, and what would make them trust it. It is expected that most would use such an exchange if the books were checked for missing pages and could be reserved online. If so, a simple exchange could lower students' costs at little expense to the council.
