Find Me Online

peer review and your research identity

Unit 29

Today's one job: review like a colleague (specific, ranked, respectful), then make sure readers can find the right you.

0

Look!

~3 min

Nodir (a fictional student) wrote a short paper on sleep and exam results. Three reviewers sent one comment each. Which one would you want to receive?

The thunderstorm

“Your statistics are a disaster. Did you even read your own tables?”

The fog

“Quite good overall, but some parts could be better. Minor revision maybe.”

The colleague

“Major issue 1: Table 2 shows that students who slept longer had higher marks, but the Discussion says that sleep causes better marks. Could this be phrased as a link rather than a cause, since the study did not test one?”

Spot it: the colleague says where (Table 2, the Discussion), why it matters (cause vs link) and what could change, and never attacks the writer.

!

The rule: rank it, place it, explain it, then be findable

SUMMARY→aim, method, main findingone real strength
MAJOR ISSUES→could the conclusions be wrong?numbered: 1, 2, 3
MINOR ISSUES→page and linewording, references, format
RECOMMENDATION→accept · minor · major · rejectthe editor decides

Find me online

ONE NAME→one Latin spellinglist the others in ORCID
ORCID iD→free, for a whole career16 characters: 0000-0002-1825-0097 (ORCID’s example record)
BIO→third person, 50–80 wordsno phone, no address

Major or minor?

~4 min

These comments are about a (fictional) survey of 40 students on mobile dictionaries. Major or minor? Circle.

1. The survey asked only students who already use mobile dictionaries, so the paper cannot compare users with non-users./
2. “Dictionary” is misspelt in the title of Table 1./
3. The conclusion says dictionaries improve vocabulary, but vocabulary was never tested./
4. Page 4, line 2: “a lot of” could be “many”./
5. The Methods section does not give the questions, so nobody could repeat the survey./
6. One source has no page numbers in the reference list./
7. The percentages in Figure 2 add up to 112 %, so some results may be wrong./
8. Figure 1 would be easier to read in black and white./

Colleague or critic?

~3 min

Which comment sounds like a colleague? Circle A or B.

1. A Your introduction is boring. B The introduction could state the research gap earlier, perhaps in the second paragraph./
2. A It is not clear from the Methods section how the 40 students were chosen. B You obviously chose your friends./
3. A Bad English everywhere!!! B Several sentences in Section 3 are very long; splitting them would help the reader./
4. A The data do not appear to support the claim on page 6. B This claim is rubbish./
5. A I recommend major revision: the findings are useful, but the conclusion needs to match the sample. B Reject./
6. A Why would anyone study this? B The paper could explain who will benefit from the findings./

Order the report

~4 min

These sentences come from one reviewer report on a (fictional) paper about online quizzes, but they are mixed up. Choose a number (1–5) for each.

1. The Discussion claims that quizzes raise marks in all subjects, but only one English course was studied.
I recommend major revision: the study is useful once the claims match the data.
The paper reports a one-term study of weekly online quizzes in an English course and finds that average quiz scores rose. The design is simple and clearly described.
3. Page 5, line 10: “quizes” should be “quizzes”.
2. The Methods section does not say how many students took part.
