Say It Your Way

in-text referencing: citing without quoting

Unit 20

Today's one job: use a source's idea in your own words and give its author the right name and year.

0

Look!

~3 min

Read the source. Then compare three ways in which students used it.

The source (fictional source for practice)

Mirzayeva (2020, p. 9): “Students who studied in rooms with plants rated their concentration higher than students who studied in identical rooms without plants, although their test scores did not differ.”

Copy-cat

Students who studied in rooms with plants rated their concentration higher than students who studied in identical rooms without plants.

Patchwork

Learners who worked in rooms with plants judged their concentration higher than learners who worked in similar rooms without plants (Mirzayeva, 2020).

Your own words

Mirzayeva (2020) found that plants in a study room made students feel more focused, although they did not raise their test scores.

Spot it: the copy-cat has no citation; the patchwork has a citation but swaps words in the source's sentence; only the last one is new words, same idea, named source.

!

7. Writing corner

Model (99 words): Green surroundings may make studying feel easier, even if they do not directly raise marks. Mirzayeva (2020) found that students working in a room with plants felt more able to concentrate than students in the same kind of room without plants, although the two groups achieved similar test results. Time outdoors may also help in stressful periods: students who regularly spent an hour or more in campus green spaces reported less exam stress than those who seldom went outside (Turgunova et al., 2023). The council could therefore recommend plants and gardens as a low-cost way to support student wellbeing.
