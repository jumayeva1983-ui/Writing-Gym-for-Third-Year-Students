Why Should I Believe You?

building academic arguments

Unit 24

Today's one job: give every claim its grounds, a warrant that links them, and an honest qualifier.

0

Look!

~3 min

Dormitory 3 tried evening “quiet hours” (20:00–22:00) for four weeks. Read the facts, then three ways of using them.

Quiet hours in Dormitory 3 (fictional data for practice)

Noise complaints fell from 18 in the four weeks before the trial to 5 during it. In a short survey, 45 of the 60 residents said that they studied more in the evening.

Just a claim

Every dormitory should have quiet hours.

Loud, not strong

Everybody knows quiet hours work! Only lazy students would disagree.

An argument

In a four-week trial, noise complaints fell from 18 to 5, and 45 of 60 residents said they studied more. Since most residents have nowhere else to study at night, quiet hours would probably help in other dormitories too, unless their residents keep very different timetables.

Spot it: the argument gives facts, says why they lead to the claim, says how sure it is and when it might not hold.

!

The rule: six questions, six parts

CLAIM→What do you want me to accept?quiet hours would help
GROUNDS→What have you got?complaints 18 → 5
WARRANT→Why does that lead to the claim?since residents need quiet to study
BACKING→Why accept the warrant?research, records, a cited source
QUALIFIER→How sure are you?probably · in most dormitories
REBUTTAL→When would it not hold?unless …

Find the hidden warrant

~5 min

Each argument hides its warrant. Which sentence links the grounds to the claim? Circle a, b or c.

1. Most students in the survey travel more than an hour to the campus. → First lectures should not start before 9:00.
a) Long journeys make very early starts hard to reach on time.
b) Travelling is bad for the environment.
c) Lectures should be shorter.//
2. An e-book reader costs less than three printed textbooks. → The faculty should lend e-book readers to first-year students.
a) E-books are modern.
b) A cheaper way to give students the same texts is worth choosing.
c) Everybody likes gadgets.//
3. Attendance at the film club doubled after it moved to Wednesdays. → Wednesday is a better day for the club.
a) Films are popular.
b) The day of the week never matters.
c) If nothing else changed, the higher attendance reflects the new day.//
4. Errors in lab reports fell after students used a checklist. → Other lab courses should use the checklist.
a) What reduced errors in these reports is likely to reduce them in similar reports.
b) Checklists are short.
c) Lab reports are boring.//
5. Most museum visitors rated the new English audio guide as useful. → The guide should also be offered in Uzbek and Russian.
a) Audio guides are cheap.
b) Visitors who prefer other languages would find a guide in their language just as useful.
c) Museums are educational.//

Which grounds give stronger support for the claim? Circle a or b.

a) My friend has never been late since it changed.

Concede and answer. Choose the form that fits the grammar and the academic register.

1. ___ the trial lasted only four weeks, complaints fell sharply.//
2. ___ the short trial, complaints fell sharply.//

~6 min

Red pen time! Rewrite each sentence as calm, fair academic English. Careful: one line is already correct!

aOf course, somebody can say that four weeks is too short, but the trial was a success.
bSeveral specialists consider quiet hours unnecessary; we cannot agree with this view at all.
cAlthough the trial was short, but the results were clear.
dIt is proved that quiet hours improve students' marks.
eWhile the trial was short, the fall in complaints was large.

Build the paragraph

~4 min

Put the argument paragraph about quiet hours in order (1–6): claim → grounds → warrant → objection → response → qualified conclusion.

It could be argued that residents studied more simply because examinations were approaching.
The trial suggests that evening quiet hours can make dormitories better places to study.
Within the limits of a short trial in one dormitory, the results support extending quiet hours to other dormitories for at least one more term.
During the four weeks, noise complaints fell from 18 to 5, and 45 of the 60 residents said that they studied more in the evening.
This cannot be excluded; however, noise complaints, which do not depend on examinations, also fell sharply.
Quiet evenings matter because most residents have no other place to study at night.

8. Writing corner

Model (129 words): The trial suggests that free water fountains can considerably reduce the use of bottled water on campus. In Building B, where three fountains were installed in March, café sales of bottled water fell from 1,200 bottles in February to 450 in April, whereas in Building C, which had no fountains, they fell only slightly, from 1,150 to 1,050. Moreover, 96 of the 120 students surveyed in Building B said that they now refill a bottle. Students who can refill a bottle nearby have little reason to buy one. It could be argued that students simply bought water elsewhere; however, the survey answers suggest that most of them changed their habits. Within the limits of a two-month trial in two buildings, the results support installing fountains in all university buildings.
