Unit 27

Today's one job: write it so that someone else could do it again: exact steps, exact numbers, exact units.

Look!

~3 min

A student club tested whether the brightness of a desk lamp changes how fast people read. Read three versions of one sentence from their report.

Lamp study (fictional study for practice)

Ten volunteers each read two texts aloud, once under a dim lamp and once under a bright lamp.

Too vague

We read some texts under a bright lamp and a dim lamp and timed it.

Unit soup

10 volunteers read 2 texts of 400 words under 300lux and 800 Lux lamps, temp. 21°C, timed in secs.

Exactly so

Ten volunteers each read two 400-word texts aloud at a desk lit at 300 lx and at 800 lx, measured at the page with a light meter. The room temperature was 21 °C. Reading time was measured with a stopwatch to the nearest second.

Spot it: the good version says who, with what, under which conditions and how it was measured, and every number has a correct unit.

First, then, once, until

~3 min

Put the steps of the lamp study in the order in which they were carried out (1–5). The small words help: first, after, until, once, then.

Reading time was measured with a stopwatch until the volunteer read the last word.
First, the light level at the page was set to 300 lx and checked with a light meter.
The second text was then read at 800 lx, and the procedure was repeated.
After a practice text of 100 words, the volunteer began to read the first test text.
Once the first text had been read, the volunteer rested for 5 min.

Accuracy, precision or uncertainty?

~3 min

Is each sentence about accuracy (close to the true value), precision (repeated results close to each other) or uncertainty (the range in which the true value probably lies)?

8. Writing corner

Model (98 words): Eight second-year students (four women and four men) took part. Each volunteer typed the same 150-word news report twice, once on a phone with a 15 cm screen and once on a laptop; the volunteers' own devices were not used. The order of the devices was decided by a coin toss, and there was a 10 min break between them. The room temperature was 21 °C to 22 °C. Typing time was measured with a stopwatch to the nearest second. Two researchers independently counted the typing errors, defined as wrong, missing or extra words, and compared their counts.
