Unit 19

Today's one job: put every sentence where the reader will look for it: why, how, what, so what.

0

Look!

~3 min

Three students observed where 40 first-year students sat in six lectures (fictional study for practice). Read three ways of reporting it.

Essay soup

Seats matter a lot! People love habits, as everybody knows. I watched some lectures. In conclusion, 31 of the 40 students kept the same seat.

Numbers only

31 kept the same seat. 12 sat in the front rows. 19 sat at the back. 9 moved around.

IMRaD map

Why? Little is known about seat choice in our faculty. How? Three observers noted where 40 first-year students sat in six lectures. What? 31 of the 40 always sat in the same area. So what? Seat habits may affect who asks questions, although six lectures is a short period.

Spot it: the map answers the reader's four questions in order, and it keeps the facts (What?) apart from their meaning (So what?).

!

The rule: four questions, four sections

INTRODUCTION→Why?area → gap → aim
METHODS→How?who, what, how analysed
RESULTS→What?facts, numbers, a table
DISCUSSION→So what?meaning, sources, limits

Three moves of an introduction

TERRITORY→the area mattersResearch has shown …
NICHE→something is missingHowever, little is known …
OCCUPY→this study fills itThe present study asks …

Danger zone

Main body as a heading ✗
because … / may … in the Results ✗
a new result in the Discussion ✗
a new source in the Conclusion ✗
No one has ever studied this ✗

Fact or meaning?

~4 min

A result says what was found. An interpretation says what it may mean. Which is each sentence?

1. Twelve of the 30 readers said that the music helped them to relax./
2. This suggests that music affects different readers in different ways./
3. Readers who chose music without words finished about as fast as in silence./
4. Songs with words probably compete with the text for attention./
5. The difference between the two conditions was less than one minute./
6. This pattern is consistent with an earlier study of school pupils (fictional source)./
7. Two participants did not finish the second text./
8. Teachers might therefore advise students to read long texts without lyrics./

Model (126 words): Quiet places for individual study are an important part of a university library, and research suggests that noise can reduce students' concentration (Ismoilova, 2021). Student surveys have also reported a growing demand for silent study spaces (Komilov, 2022). However, little is known about who actually uses such spaces once they are opened, or why. Last year our library opened a silent study room, but its users have not yet been asked about their reasons. The present study addresses this question through a questionnaire completed by 50 users of the room. It asks who uses the room, how often, and for what purposes. The results show that most users came to prepare for examinations. The paper first describes the questionnaire and then presents and discusses the results. (Both sources are fictional.)
