Writing Gym · Module 3 · Unit 26

Assistant, Not Author

using AI tools responsibly

Unit 26

Today's one job: let an AI tool assist you, never author for you: ask it to help you learn, check what it says, and say what it did.

0

Look!

~3 min

Sardor has to write a 200-word reflection on a museum visit for his history seminar. Three ways of using the same chatbot (QuickQuill, a fictional tool):

The ghostwriter

Sardor pastes the task into the chatbot, changes three words in its answer and submits it. He says nothing.

The secret helper

Sardor writes the reflection himself, then lets the chatbot rewrite every sentence “to sound better”. He does not mention it.

The assistant

Sardor writes it himself and asks the chatbot to mark unclear sentences without fixing them. He rewrites four of them and adds one line: “A chatbot marked four unclear sentences; I revised them myself.”

Spot it: in the third version Sardor does all the writing, decides about every suggestion and says exactly what the tool did.

!

The rule: ask, check, decide, declare

ASK→to learn, not to replace“Mark it, don't fix it”
CHECK→Does it exist?Does it say this?Is it current?
DECIDE→keep · correct · discard
DECLARE→which toolwhat it didwhat you did

The traffic light

GREEN→explains · checks · quizzesyou make every decision
AMBER→shapes ideas, structure or wordingonly if allowed and declared
RED→does the work you are marked onor invents something

Order the checks

~3 min

A chatbot told Jamshid that a study found “students revise more after peer feedback”. Put his checks in order (1–5).

Read the passage the chatbot summarised and compare it with the claim.
Note in your AI log what you checked and what you decided.
Find the original study in the library database.
Rewrite the claim in your own words and cite the study, not the chatbot.
Check the date and look for newer studies on the same question.
